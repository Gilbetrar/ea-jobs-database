###  [Informing Policy using Micro-Simulations](/articles/nikhil-woodruff-micro-simulation) 
 
 
      
 
 Nikhil Woodruff  
 BSc  
  
 SECONDED FROM  
        
 

Policy analysis in government is built on decades of experience and practice, and spread out in pieces over departments. This means we have high-quality final analysis, but when options are being weighed up, the fragmentation and pace can often fail to meet the needs of decision makers. And because of this, they often don't have all the analysis they need or want when they're considering what to do.

###  [Transforming Justice with Responsible AI](/articles/william-tarr-moj) 
 
 
      
 
 William Tarr  
  
 [  ](https://www.linkedin.com/in/willtarr/)  
 PREVIOUSLY  
        
 

Improving the efficiency of public sector workers is a perennial objective for civil servants and politicians, but one for which the root causes are widely misunderstood. A persistent assumption is that the frontline service itself is predisposed to lag behind the productivity of the private sector. This is particularly true for prisons. From the outside, it must seem a puzzle as to how prison officers performing essentially identical custodial roles can operate with markedly varying efficiencies under different management. What is hidden from public view, however, is the scale of the documentation burden imposed on every layer of prison governance. Deploying software engineers practiced in the deployment of AI, though, seems our best method of tackling it.
