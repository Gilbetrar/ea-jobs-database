# **GovAI is Seeking a Director of Operations**

# Key Points

## What the Role Would Involve

**We are looking for a new leader for our operations team.** This person would work closely with Georg Arndt (GovAI's Chief of Staff) and Ben Garfinkel (GovAI's Executive Director) to:

- Lead and grow GovAI's Operations teams (currently 13 FTEs).
- Shepherd GovAI through its next phase of growth as an organization (from ~45 to potentially 100 FTE within the next couple of years, with application volumes for our seasonal fellowships likely exceeding 20,000 per year).
- Oversee the development and execution of excellent operational processes and projects.
- Spearhead strategic priorities for GovAI.

Depending on the role holder's interests and skills, a number of additional functions could sit under their branch of the organization. These include community management and partnerships, fundraising, or communications. In its most expansive form, the role would involve close engagement with org-level strategy development and ownership over new strategic initiatives.

# Further Context

## GovAI's Existing Activities and Impact

GovAI's mission is to positively influence the lasting impact of AI, by helping key institutions make better decisions. We do that by (1) producing useful research and guidance and (2) developing and placing talent who can work in or help to inform these institutions.

We see ourselves as both a research organization and a talent development organization, with these two functions playing synergistic roles. We have an exceptional impact track so far – and expect our impact to grow in the coming years.

### Career Development

Our key **career development** activities are:

- We run three-month, in-person research fellowships, both [in the UK](https://www.governance.ai/post/summer-fellowship-2026-research-track) (40+ people per cohort) and [in DC](https://www.governance.ai/post/dc-summer-fellowship-2026) (~20 people per cohort).
- We offer one-year [research scholar positions](https://www.governance.ai/post/research-scholar), to support career development and career transitions (~10 people at a time).
- We run a bi-annual online policy program, which brings together participants primarily based in DC, and is designed to deepen understanding of AI policy in the US.
- We support part-time and full-time secondments (e.g. we currently have a staff member seconded to the UK Prime Minister's office).
- We are also unusually supportive of staff pursuing further career opportunities.

This year, we are also running an experimental pilot for an [applied track](https://www.governance.ai/post/summer-fellowship-2026-applied-track) of our UK seasonal fellowship to address the growing need for non-research specialists in the field.

We may run more pilots in the future, in addition to continuing to scale and improve our existing programs. We are partly responding to rapidly growing demand for our programs, with (e.g.) **applications for our summer fellowship program growing from roughly 80 to roughly 8,000 over three years**.

### Research and Advising

We produce a combination of reports, academic papers, and non-public research and advising of various kinds. We directly engage with a range of frontier AI companies, government bodies, and other organizations.

In the past few years, some particularly notable work includes:

- Our Director of Policy and Research has served as [vice-chair](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/meet-chairs-leading-development-first-general-purpose-ai-code-practice) for a "code of practice" working group for the EU AI Act, helping to write the text that clarifies the safety and security practices frontier AI companies must adopt to put their models on the European market
- Team members have engaged with frontier AI companies as they have revised and begun to implement their "safety frameworks," with apparent influence in some cases. Among other contributions, members of our threat modeling team have informed senior stakeholders' thinking on cyberoffensive and biosecurity risks.[^2]
- Team members have produced or co-authored foundational papers on a number of emerging topics, including the first major report on "[technical governance](https://cdn.governance.ai/Open_Problems_in_Technical_AI_Governance.pdf)", the first paper articulating the concept of / need for "[agent infrastructure](https://www.governance.ai/research-paper/infrastructure-for-ai-agents)", the first description and definition of "[cooperative AI](https://www.governance.ai/research-paper/open-problems-in-cooperative-ai)", and helping to establish the concept of [frontier AI regulation](https://www.governance.ai/analysis/frontier-ai-regulation).

We expect to significantly scale up our research activity over the next year and pilot new workstreams. We are currently expanding our US policy presence: this has included adding our first three DC-based researchers in the past year (one of them the most recent head of the primary DoD agency tasked with reducing threats from WMDs) and setting up a DC office.

We are also currently building up an economics of AI workstream to take advantage of growing interest in the topic; our econ work is now attracting increasing attention, including reachouts from Senate offices and a recent article on the front page of the [Washington Post online](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/interactive/2026/jobs-most-affected-ai-automation/).

We will also potentially set up a China workstream this year, in addition to expanding some existing workstreams.

## GovAI's Next Organizational "Era"

GovAI has been growing rapidly, increasing from three core staff at the end of 2021 to ~45 staff members at present (plus more than 130 seasonal fellows per year).

We are now entering a new era of growth, which will involve both a further increase in scale and the addition of new programs and workstreams.

This growth will create opportunities, but also significant operational challenges. Some dimensions of growth and associated challenges are:

- **Growing team**: We are on a trajectory to grow from 45 to almost 100 staff in 2 years. This will come with the challenge of building structures to support coordination and maintain quality standards and culture, while also avoiding the risks of excessive bureaucracy.
- **Growing interest**: We are managing increasingly large application volumes for our programs, with the candidates for our fellowship cohorts growing from 80 to over 8,000 in 3 years. This comes with the challenge of designing our selection processes in a way that allows us to identify the best candidates in the pool (including ones other orgs might overlook), without placing an excessive burden on our staff.
- **Growing audience**: We are advising a wide and growing range of private and public institutions across the world, which adds significant new opportunities for impact but requires careful navigation to avoid reputational and legal risks.
- **Growing portfolio**: We are running new programs (e.g. a DC-based fellowship and DC office expansion), exploring new high-impact activities, and adding new functions (e.g. community management and partnerships) to the organization. These will all require strategic input as well as new systems and infrastructure.
- **Growing geographic scope**: It is likely we will increasingly be operating across multiple jurisdictions, including the UK, US, and EU. This may even ultimately involve the creation of new, partly-independent initiatives under the GovAI umbrella that have different geographic focuses. Coordinating effective decision-making, risk management, communications, and operational support across regions will be critical and complicated.

## The Leverage of Operational Excellence

As we grow, excellent operations work will be critical in a number of ways.

- It will ensure that we have the capacity and skill necessary to feel comfortable piloting new projects.
- It will ensure that we will be able to maintain our core activities at (at least) their current quality and pace, even as the amount of work that needs to happen grows.
  - For example – as the number of program applications we receive and selection decisions we make grows exponentially – good operations work is necessary to ensure that we continue to pick the best candidates from these pools, without placing an excessive burden on our staff.
- It will ensure that GovAI's leadership can focus on progressing additional long-term priorities, due to trust in the team's ability to reliably execute major responsibilities with limited oversight.
- It will reinforce our reputation as highly competent and reliable partners by delivering excellent events, clear communications, and a strong online presence to external stakeholders in government, industry, and civil society.
- It will (as much as possible) maintain coordination and culture inside the organisation, limit bureaucratic friction, and generally reduce the risks and costs that naturally come with growth.

A strong leader of the operations team can be a force multiplier on the entire organization. As the organization grows, there will continue to be a steady stream of new challenges that must be tackled, and it's likely the impact of this role will grow alongside it.
