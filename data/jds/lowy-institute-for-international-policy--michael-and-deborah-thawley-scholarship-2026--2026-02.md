##  Research Fellow, Aid and Development Policy — Indo-Pacific Development Centre 
 
 
 
 

The Lowy Institute is currently recruiting for a **Research Fellow** within its Indo-Pacific Development Centre (IPDC).

### About the role

The IPDC focuses on the challenges facing developing economies in Southeast Asia, the Pacific, and South Asia and the role of international development partners. We are seeking a Research Fellow with strong expertise in aid and development policy in the Indo-Pacific region.

The Research Fellow will be the lead researcher for the [Southeast Asia Aid Map](https://seamap.lowyinstitute.org/) — a flagship interactive data-based research product and platform tracing and analysing development finance across Southeast Asia. The Southeast Asia Aid Map is a high-profile research product with significant policy relevance and public reach. Beyond these core responsibilities, the Research Fellow will be expected to undertake significant topical research, analysis, and commentary on issues within their areas of expertise affecting Southeast Asia and potentially the Pacific and South Asia where relevant.

This is an opportunity for an experienced researcher or practitioner to assume a leading role in a high-impact team within a collaborative and multidisciplinary culture, and to take ownership of an established and widely recognised research product.

### Key responsibilities

Under the overall guidance of the IPDC Director, the Research Fellow will:

- Lead the research and analytical direction of the Southeast Asia Aid Map, including overseeing data systems, methodologies, and the production of high-impact key findings reports, topical research, and public-facing outputs
- Contribute to the Pacific Aid Map as required supporting its research and analytical outputs
- Produce original, agenda-setting policy research, analysis, and commentary on aid and development issues in the Indo-Pacific
- Supervise and support junior researchers, including overseeing detailed data work
- Engage in outreach activities including events, presentations, podcasts, and media
- Engage in Australia and internationally with senior policymakers, development practitioners, the media, and other key stakeholders
- Contribute their expertise to the wider IPDC and Lowy Institute research team

While the position is primarily desk-based, occasional domestic and international travel for research, events, and stakeholder engagement will be required.

### About you

The ideal candidate will combine strong academic credentials with practical experience in the aid and development field. Experience as a policymaker and/or development practitioner is highly valued and forms part of the ideal candidate profile. Relevant academic research is also valued. A strong interest in evidence-based development policy in Southeast Asia and the broader Indo-Pacific region is essential.

Specifically, candidates should demonstrate:

- Well-developed knowledge of aid and development effectiveness, including familiarity with key debates and policy frameworks
- Extensive experience working on development-related issues in the Indo-Pacific region, whether in research, government, or as a practitioner
- Proficient data capabilities, including comfort working with large datasets and the ability to both undertake and oversee detailed data work
- Excellent communication skills, both written and oral, including the ability to present to and engage with senior policymakers and the media
- A collegiate and collaborative working style

Advanced proficiency in Excel and familiarity with other data tools or programming languages is highly desirable, as are strong organisational and project management capabilities.

We aim to build a strong and diverse research team with a broad mix of skills, perspectives, and analytical strengths. Women and candidates from diverse backgrounds are especially encouraged to apply. The successful candidate will join the organisation at a level commensurate with experience, and with salary and benefits set accordingly.

### How to apply

**To apply, please send your CV and a short cover letter to **[**recruit@lowyinstitute.org**](mailto:recruit@lowyinstitute.org)** by 27 March 2026.**

For additional information regarding the role, please contact Lowy Institute recruitment via the email above.

##  Research Associate, Southeast Asia Aid Map — Indo-Pacific Development Centre 
 
 
 
 

The Lowy Institute is currently recruiting for a **Research Associate** within its Indo-Pacific Development Centre (IPDC).

### About the role

The IPDC focuses on the challenges facing developing economies in Southeast Asia, the Pacific, and South Asia and the role of international development partners. The Research Associate will work on the [Southeast Asia Aid Map](https://seamap.lowyinstitute.org/) — a flagship interactive data-based research product and platform tracing and analysing development finance across Southeast Asia.

Compiling, managing, and analysing data on foreign aid flows in Southeast Asia, the Research Associate will contribute to the production of high-impact research and public-facing outputs, and support substantial topical analysis related to aid and development issues in the region.

This is an excellent opportunity for an early-career researcher to join a high-impact team within a collaborative and multidisciplinary culture, and to contribute to an established and widely recognised research product.

### Key responsibilities

Working under the guidance of the IPDC's lead researcher for the Southeast Asia Aid Map and the IPDC Director, the Research Associate will:

- Compile, verify, and analyse data on development finance in Southeast Asia, working with large and complex datasets
- Support the production of key findings reports, topical research, and other public-facing outputs
- Contribute to policy analysis and commentary on aid and development issues in Southeast Asia
- Support the delivery of events, outreach activities, and stakeholder engagement

While the position is primarily desk-based, occasional domestic and international travel for research, events, and stakeholder engagement may be required, particularly to source, verify, and analyse foreign aid data.

##  2026 Michael and Deborah Thawley Scholarship 
 
 
 
 

The Michael and Deborah Thawley Scholarship offers an Australian official, officer, student, or recent graduate an exceptional opportunity to contribute to the work of two leading think tanks, the Lowy Institute and the Center for Strategic and International Studies.

### The Scholarship

The scholarship provides direct exposure to the work of both organisations, at the intersection of research and policy in international affairs. The Thawley Scholar will work for a short period in the International Security Program at the Lowy Institute in Sydney, and spend 8–12 weeks in Washington DC at the Center for Strategic and International Studies.

Research to be carried out by the Thawley Scholar is flexible but is expected to place a strong emphasis on the regional and geopolitical dimensions of Australia’s strategic interests. The purpose of the scholarship is to encourage Australian thinkers who can examine the world from the perspective of a superpower, and thereby understand Australia’s place in the global system.

The CSIS placement offers a rare chance to gain direct insight into US debates on global strategic challenges and policy responses, including opportunities to interact with the Washington foreign policy establishment and to meet with high-level policymakers and commentators, and then to apply that knowledge professionally upon return to Australia.

The successful applicant will receive a A$10,000 award as well as a two-stage research placement in Sydney and Washington DC. In addition to the award, flights and a travel allowance are provided for the Washington placement. Domestic travel for the Sydney placement will be considered on a case-by-case basis.

The timing and length of the placement is flexible within the 2025 calendar year, and a start date will be negotiated with the successful candidate.

### Applications

Applications should consist of a short cover letter that includes a one-paragraph research agenda, a CV, and an 800-word draft article that might be suitable for publication in the Lowy Institute’s digital magazine, *The Interpreter* (applicants may submit previously published articles).

Please submit your application to the Director of the International Security Program, **Sam Roggeveen** ([sroggeveen@lowyinstitute.org](mailto:sroggeveen@lowyinstitute.org)). Applications close at COB on 2 April 2026.

### Eligibility Requirements

There is no specific professional profile for the preferred applicant, but a competitive candidate will typically be either a promising junior to mid-career Australian Government official or Australian Defence Force officer, or an exceptional Australian postgraduate student with wider experience and demonstrated interests in international security. Please note, this scholarship is only open to Australian citizens and holders of permanent residency.

A list of [past Thawley scholars can be found here](/programs-projects/michael-deborah-thawley-scholarship-international-security).
